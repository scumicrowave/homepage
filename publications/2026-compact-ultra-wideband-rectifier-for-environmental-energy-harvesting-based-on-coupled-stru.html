Compact Ultra-Wideband Rectifier for Environmental Energy Harvesting Based on Coupled Structure

Abstract

This paper presents a compact, ultra-wideband, and high-efficiency rectifier designed for microwave power transmission and energy harvesting applications. The proposed architecture integrates a series-coupled matching network with cascading band-stop structures, enabling both impedance transformation and harmonic control within a compact footprint. Two highly compact rectifiers are designed, fabricated, and measured to verify the proposed method. A broadband rectifier, operating between 1.8 and 3.0 GHz with a relative bandwidth of 50%, achieves above 70% rectification efficiency and a peak efficiency of 79%. The other wideband operation mechanism is analytically investigated: the band-stop network reshapes the diode’s impedance profile by tuning its low- and high-frequency components into a conjugate rela- tionship, while the coupled matching structure transforms the resulting impedance to match the system impedance, thereby realizing ultra-wideband matching. The fabricated rectifier achieves a measured efficiency exceeding 40% across a wide frequency range of 1.1-3.9 GHz, corresponding to a 112% relative bandwidth at a low input power level of 0 dBm. In addition to its wideband and efficient performance, the rectifier features a highly compact form factor of only 18 × 24.8 mm2. Introduction As an emerging wireless power transmission technology, microwave power transmission (MPT) and energy harvesting (EH) have attracted significant research attention due to their numerous advantages [1, 2]. These technologies offer a promising solution to the critical limitation of bat- tery lifespan in wireless sensor networks (WSNs) [3, 4], thereby significantly improving power availability and enabling the broader deployment of WSNs across various application scenarios [5-7]. Within MPT and EH systems, two persistent challenges are maximizing the capture of inci- dent electromagnetic energy and improving the radio frequency to direct current (RF-to-DC) conversion efficiency [8, 9]. A significant challenge in achieving high-efficiency wideband recti- fication is the nonlinear and frequency-dependent input impedance of the rectifying diode. This nonlinearity complicates the impedance matching process across a broad frequency range. To address this challenge, non-uniform transmission lines have been effectively employed to real- ize broadband impedance matching, enabling rectifiers to maintain high conversion efficiency over extended bandwidths [10, 11].
